Research

I study labor markets, public policy, and inequality, with a focus on migration, trade, and enforcement.

Published

Does the Enforcement of Labor Regulations Reduce Informality? The Case of Peru

with María Florencia Pinto and Hernán Winkler. Latin American Economic Review 37 (2026). doi

We study stronger labor inspections in Peru from 2010 to 2019, using the creation of SUNAFIL and its gradual rollout. Enforcement increased fines on formal firms, but we find little evidence of meaningful changes in employment or informality.

Working papers

Expanding Lawful Pathways: The Economic Impact of New Migration Policies in the U.S.

Doctoral research

This paper asks how deportation protection and work authorization affect migrants and native workers, using expansions of Temporary Protected Status (TPS) to study labor-market responses.

Child Mortality and the Unexpected Effect of Deep Trade Agreements

with Alberto Chong and Alejandro Riaño

Using staggered adoption of Free Trade Agreements from 1990 to 2019, we find that earlier unilateral liberalization is associated with declining child mortality, while deeper FTAs are associated with a slower decline. Effects vary by timing, children’s age, and FTA characteristics.

Presented at City, University of London; University of Surrey; Brunel University; and LACEA-LAMES 2022.
